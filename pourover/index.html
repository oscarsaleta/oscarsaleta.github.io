General Brewing Variables Explained ▼

Recipe Structure: A consistent two-pour approach (bloom followed by one or two laminar circular pours) minimizes confounding variables.

Ratio: Ratio controls extraction yield and strength. Tighter ratios (1:15) reduce the amount of water solvent passing through the coffee bed, preventing harsh astringency in darker roasts.

Bloom: Wetting the coffee slurry with 2.5x–3x dose releases trapped CO2 gas from roasting cells. Un-degassed coffee repels water and causes channelled extraction.

Agitation: Swirling or high-kettle pour turbulence breaks dry pockets but increases fines migration to the filter paper bottom, risking stalling.

Advanced Considerations: Water Chemistry, Fines & Filters ▼

Water Composition: Hard water with excessive buffer capacity (alkalinity) mutes delicate citric/malic acidity. Aim for 40-75 ppm General Hardness and 20-40 ppm buffer.

Filter Paper Porosity: Faster flowing papers (e.g. Cafec Abaca or T-90) allow finer grinds without stalling the bed.

Roast Age: Ultra-dense light roasts often hit peak flavor clarity 14 to 30 days post-roast, once organic degassing stabilizes.
