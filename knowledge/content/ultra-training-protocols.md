# Ultra Marathon Fueling & Gut Training Protocols

Comprehensive endurance physiology, exogenous carbohydrate intake rates, sodium balance, and stomach conditioning strategies for 50km to 100km ultramarathons.

---

## The Metabolic Bottleneck

In events exceeding 3 hours, glycogen depletion is the primary determinant of peripheral fatigue and central nervous system drag. The human body stores approximately:
- **Liver glycogen:** ~80-100g (~400 kcal)
- **Muscle glycogen:** ~400-500g (~1,600-2,000 kcal)
- **Total accessible glycogen:** ~2,000-2,400 kcal

At ultra marathon paces, energy expenditure often averages 600–900 kcal/hour depending on grade, body mass, and terrain. Exogenous fueling is not optional; it dictates whether you finish strong or face severe DNF risk.

---

## Carbohydrate Absorption Rates

Traditional guidelines capped carb absorption at 60g/hour. Modern sports nutrition has expanded this via dual-transport mechanisms:

| Transporter | Sugar Substrate | Max Absorption Rate |
|---|---|---|
| **SGLT1** | Glucose / Maltodextrin | ~60g / hour |
| **GLUT5** | Fructose | ~30g - 50g / hour |
| **Combined** | **1:0.8 to 2:1 Ratio** | **90g - 120g / hour** |

> **Key Rule**: Never rely solely on glucose or maltodextrin above 60g/hr. Always use a 1:0.8 or 2:1 Glucose:Fructose ratio to exploit multiple intestinal transport pathways without saturating SGLT1.

---

## Gut Training Protocol

Reaching 90g/hr without GI distress requires progressive gut adaptations over 6–10 weeks prior to race day:

1. **Weeks 1–3**: 50–60g/hr on all runs over 90 minutes.
2. **Weeks 4–6**: Increase to 70–80g/hr on weekend long runs and race-pace tempos.
3. **Weeks 7–9**: Target race-day target of 90g/hr with planned race-day gels, chews, and drink mix. Practice fluid volume concurrently (600–800ml/hr).
4. **Race Week**: Taper volume; maintain standard balanced meals with moderate carb loading (8-10g/kg body weight) for 36-48h before start.

## Hydration & Sodium Balance

- **Sweat Sodium Concentration**: Averages 800–1,200mg sodium per liter of sweat.
- **Replacement Target**: Aim for 500–800mg sodium per 750ml of fluid in moderate heat, scaling up to 1,000mg/L in high heat/humidity.
- **Hyponatremia Warning**: Never consume pure water in large volumes without electrolytes during prolonged exertion. Over-drinking plain water dilutes blood serum sodium, causing dangerous exercise-associated hyponatremia (EAH).

---

## Race Day Aid Station Strategy

- **Bag drop checklist**: Spare soft flasks with pre-portioned powder, salt caps, blister kit, emergency caffeine gel (100mg) for the final 25km.
- **Palate Fatigue Contingency**: Sweetness exhaustion is common after 6 hours. Pack savory options: salted boiled potatoes, ginger chews, pretzels, and savory rice cakes.
