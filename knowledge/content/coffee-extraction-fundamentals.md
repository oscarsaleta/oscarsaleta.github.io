# Coffee Extraction & Dial-in Fundamentals

A technical breakdown of Total Dissolved Solids (TDS), Extraction Yield (EY), agitation dynamics, and brew parameter adjustments for specialty pour-over.

---

## The Extraction Matrix

Coffee extraction is governed by solubility rates of organic compounds in roasted coffee beans:

1. **Fats & Acids (Fast extraction)**: Citric, malic, and phosphoric acids extract almost instantly. Under-extracted coffee tastes sour, sharp, and hollow.
2. **Sugars & Aromatics (Mid extraction)**: Caramelized sugars, floral and fruity volatiles. Creates sweetness, balance, and complex body.
3. **Plant Fibers & Polyphenols (Slow extraction)**: Bitter compounds, astringent tannins, and chlorogenic acid breakdown products. Over-extraction yields dryness and harsh bitterness.

---

## Key Formulas

$$\text{Extraction Yield \%} = \frac{\text{Brewed Coffee Mass (g)} \times \text{TDS \%}}{\text{Dry Dose (g)}}$$

- **Target Extraction Yield**: 18% – 22% (ideal sweet spot for light to medium specialty roasts).
- **Target TDS**: 1.25% – 1.45% for filter coffee.

## Dial-in Heuristics (Troubleshooting Compass)

When tasting your cup, follow this diagnostic loop:

```
Tasting Cup
  │
  ├─► Sour, sharp, salty, watery body?
  │     └─ Under-extracted!
  │          Actions: Grind finer, increase brew water temp (e.g. 93°C -> 98°C), 
  │                   or add a gentle swirl/agitation during bloom.
  │
  ├─► Astringent, chalky, bitter aftertaste, drying finish?
  │     └─ Over-extracted / Channelling!
  │          Actions: Grind coarser, reduce agitation/swirling, 
  │                   or lower brew water temp (e.g. 95°C -> 90°C).
  │
  └─► Sweet, vibrant acidity, clean finish, complex aromatics?
        └─ Dialled in! Lock down your recipe parameters.
```

## Pour Structure & Agitation Mechanics

- **Bloom Stage**:
  - Dose water at 2.5x to 3x dry coffee weight.
  - Duration: 40–45s for standard coffees, 60s for ultra-fresh/dense high-elevation coffees.
  - Purpose: Expels carbon dioxide trapped in cellular structures so subsequent water penetrates evenly.

- **Pour Velocity & Height**:
  - Higher pour = higher kinetic energy = deeper bed disturbance and more fines migration (risk of clogging filter papers).
  - Gentle circular spiral pours from 5–8cm height maintain uniform laminar percolation.
