# Coffee Extraction & Dial-in Fundamentals (Lance Hedrick Method)

A rigorous breakdown of extraction physics, Total Dissolved Solids (TDS), Extraction Yield (EY), channelling dynamics, and the sensory dial-in compass for specialty pour-over.

---

## Lance's 4 Golden Rules of Dialing In

1. **Brew Time is an Output, Never a Target**:
   - Drawdown time is purely a symptom of bean density, grind distribution, fines generation, and filter paper porosity.
   - Never coarsen or fine your grind just to hit an arbitrary clock (e.g. 3:00 minutes). If a brew finishes at 2:15 or 4:00 and tastes sweet, clean, and vibrant, it is dialed in.
2. **Lock Your Foundation (One Variable at a Time)**:
   - Fix your Dose (15g or 20g), Ratio (1:16 baseline), Water Chemistry, and Pour Structure.
   - **Adjust Grind Size first**. Only touch secondary variables (water temperature, agitation, ratio) once grind size is dialed or if astringency prevents grinding finer.
3. **Astringency ≠ Bitterness**:
   - Bitterness is a *taste sensation* (medicinal, ashy, roasted compounds).
   - Astringency is a *tactile defect* (mouth-drying sandpaper, chalky coating on cheeks and tongue) caused by polyphenols extracted through micro-channels when fines choke filter paper pores.
4. **Evaluate as the Cup Cools (50°C – 60°C)**:
   - Piping hot coffee burns oral thermoreceptors and masks volatile organic aromatics and defects.
   - Always taste the cup at hot, warm, and cool stages; the true balance between acidity and sweetness reveals itself below 60°C.

## The Extraction Matrix

Organic compounds extract in a distinct chronological and kinetic sequence:

1. **Fats & Organic Acids (Immediate extraction)**: Citric, malic, and phosphoric acids extract almost instantly. Under-extracted coffee tastes sharp, sour, salty, and hollow.
2. **Caramelized Sugars (Mid extraction)**: Fructose, glucose, and caramelized sucrose. Creates sweetness, body, balance, and rounded mouthfeel.
3. **Heavy Polyphenols & Tannins (Slow extraction)**: Astringent polyphenols and chlorogenic acid breakdown products.

---

## The Channelling Trap: Why Finer Isn't Always Higher Extraction

When you grind excessively fine or swirl aggressively:
1. Micro-fines migrate down to the filter paper bottom and form a compacted silt seal.
2. Water cannot permeate the clogged bed uniformly and seeks the path of least resistance.
3. Water carves high-velocity fissures (**channels**) through the bed.
4. **The result:** Grounds inside the channels are severely over-extracted and stripped of astringent tannins, while bypassed grounds remain under-extracted and sour. The cup tastes both harsh/drying and sour/empty at once.

## Sensory Diagnostic Compass

```
Sensory Evaluation
  │
  ├─► Sour, sharp, salty, lacks sweetness?
  │     └─ Under-extracted!
  │          Action: Grind 1–2 clicks finer.
  │          Secondary: Increase water temp to 98°C–100°C.
  │
  ├─► Astringent, chalky, sandpaper drying sensation on tongue?
  │     └─ Channelling / Fines clogging!
  │          Action: Grind 1–2 clicks coarser immediately.
  │          Secondary: Eliminate aggressive kettle pouring and drawdown swirling.
  │
  ├─► Bitter, ashy, heavy back-of-throat burn?
  │     └─ Over-extracted solubles!
  │          Action: Grind 1 click coarser, or lower water temp (e.g. 94°C → 91°C).
  │          Secondary: Tighten ratio slightly (1:16 → 1:15).
  │
  ├─► Weak, thin, diluted body?
  │     └─ Low TDS / under-strength!
  │          Action: Tighten brew ratio (1:16 → 1:15.5) or increase dose slightly.
  │
  └─► Sweet, vibrant acidity, juicy, long clean aftertaste?
        └─ Dialed In! 🌟 Lock down recipe parameters in your journal.
```

## Lance's Universal Two-Pour Recipe

- **Ratio**: 1:16 (15g coffee : 240g water or 20g : 320g water)
- **Water Temp**: 98°C–100°C for light roasts; 92°C–94°C for medium roasts; 86°C–88°C for dark/decaf.
- **Phase 1: Bloom (0:00 - 0:45)**:
  - Dose 3x coffee weight in water (45g for 15g dose; 60g for 20g dose).
  - Swirl or excavate bed immediately to ensure zero dry clumps.
  - Let degas until 0:45.
- **Phase 2: Main Pour (0:45 - 1:15)**:
  - Single continuous laminar pour in gentle concentric circles at low height.
  - Pour until scale hits final target brew weight (e.g. 240g or 320g).
- **Phase 3: Settling & Drawdown (1:15+)**:
  - Give a single gentle swirl to wash down clinging grounds and settle a flat bed.
  - Hands off; allow gravity to finish drawdown cleanly.
